Why .com is the default choice

The .com extension is the one most people type from memory and the one international customers recognise instantly. If your business sells outside Pakistan, or you simply want the safest, most familiar address for your website, .com is the default. It is open to anyone in any country — there is no document or local-address requirement the way some country extensions have. A .pk domain, by contrast, signals a Pakistani business and suits brands whose customers are mainly in Pakistan. Many businesses end up registering both: .com for the wider audience and .pk for the local one. If you want the local option, see how to register a .pk domain in Pakistan.

First-year price vs renewal price — the difference that matters

Almost every registrar shows two prices for a domain: the price for the first year and the price to renew it in the second year. The first-year price is often lower — a promotional rate to get you started — while the renewal price is the regular rate you will pay every year after that. A domain that looks cheap in year one can cost far more in year two if you only looked at the first number.

Before you pay, always do this: on the checkout page, look for the renewal price next to the registration price, and work out what the domain will cost you over two or three years, not just the first. If a seller hides the renewal price until after you have paid, that is a warning sign. At ZedgeHost, check the renewal price shown at checkout before you complete the order, and keep the renewal date in your calendar so the domain never lapses by accident.

How to buy a .com domain in 5 steps

  1. Search for the name. Go to the domain search page and type the name you want — your business name, your brand, or the shortest version of it you can get. Try it without hyphens and numbers first: short, plain names are easier to remember, say on the phone, and print on a card.
  2. Check availability — and have a backup. If your exact name is taken, do not buy a long, awkward version of it just to keep the .com. Try a short variation (add a word like “pk” or your city), or check whether the .pk version is free. A clean name on a different extension beats a confusing name on .com.
  3. Register it in your own name. Create your account and enter your own name, email address and phone number as the domain owner. This matters more than people think: the name on the registration is the proof of ownership if there is ever a dispute, a transfer, or a renewal problem. Never let the domain be registered in someone else’s name.
  4. Pay in PKR. ZedgeHost accepts Easypaisa, JazzCash, Raast and bank transfer, so you pay in rupees with the wallet or bank you already use. There is no need for an international credit card and no conversion fee. See how ordering works before you buy, so you know each step from choosing a name to the domain going live.
  5. Verify your email and point the domain at your hosting. After registration, ICANN rules require you to verify the owner’s email address — click the verification link sent to your inbox, or the domain can be suspended. Then point the domain at your hosting with DNS settings (nameservers or an A record) so your website loads on it. What to check before you buy

  • The renewal price. Check it at checkout, not after you have paid. Work out the two- or three-year cost before you commit.
  • Ownership in your name. The domain must be registered in your own name, with your own email and phone number. That is your proof of ownership.
  • Full DNS control. Make sure you can manage the domain’s DNS settings yourself, so you can point it at any hosting provider — not only the one that sold it to you.
  • Local payment. Confirm you can pay in PKR with Easypaisa, JazzCash, Raast or bank transfer, without an international card or conversion fee.
  • Transfer rules. A newly registered or freshly transferred .com is normally locked against another transfer for 60 days under ICANN rules. That is normal — it protects the domain from being moved without the owner’s knowledge — but know it before you plan a move.
  • Renewal reminders. A domain expires if it is not renewed. Keep the renewal date in your calendar and keep your contact email up to date so the reminders reach you.
