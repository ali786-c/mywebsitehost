Why paying in PKR matters

For most buyers in Pakistan, the hardest part of buying hosting has never been choosing a plan — it has been paying for one. International hosts bill in dollars and expect a credit card, which many students, freelancers and small shop owners here do not have. Even when a card works, the bank adds a conversion charge and the final amount is never quite the price shown on the page.

Paying in PKR changes that completely. The price you see is the price you pay: Rs 1,499 means Rs 1,499 leaves your wallet — not Rs 1,499 plus a conversion fee plus tax on the conversion. JazzCash and Easypaisa are already how most Pakistanis pay for everyday things, so paying for hosting the same way needs no new account, no bank visit and no card application. JazzCash vs Easypaisa — which one should you use?

For a hosting invoice, there is no practical difference between the two: both are PKR wallets, both pay the same invoice total, and both need the same confirmation step afterwards. Use whichever wallet you already have money in. The only thing that matters is the wallet’s daily transfer limit, which is set by the wallet provider, not by the host. A shared hosting invoice of Rs 1,499 or Rs 3,999 fits comfortably inside a normal wallet limit; a large VPS or Enterprise invoice may be above it, in which case bank transfer is the simpler option.

If you also need a domain, the same payment methods work there too: a .com domain at ZedgeHost costs Rs 2,999 for the first year (renews at Rs 3,999/year), payable in PKR with JazzCash, Easypaisa, Raast or bank transfer — see how to buy a cheap .com domain in Pakistan and the domain search page.

Paying renewals with JazzCash or Easypaisa

Renewals work exactly like first orders: when your hosting is due, you get a renewal invoice in PKR and pay it with JazzCash, Easypaisa, Raast or bank transfer. Two habits keep renewals painless. First, pay before the due date — a hosting account can be suspended if the invoice stays unpaid past it, and a lapsed domain is worse, because someone else can register it. Second, keep your invoice number and transaction ID until the payment shows as confirmed in the client area. If the amount on the renewal invoice surprises you, check it against the plan price before paying — and remember that a plan’s renewal price is the price that applies every year after the first, so it is worth knowing before you commit to a plan, not after.

What to check before you pay

  • The invoice total in PKR. Confirm the amount matches the plan price — Rs 1,499, Rs 2,999, Rs 3,999 or Rs 9,999 per year for shared/WordPress hosting — before you send anything.
  • Your invoice number. Every payment must be matched to an invoice. Keep the number with the transaction ID so billing can find your payment quickly.
  • The exact amount, not a round figure. Send the total shown on the invoice. A short payment delays activation until the balance is cleared.
  • The payment details on your own invoice. Pay using the details shown in your client area invoice — not a number copied from a random page, chat or comment. If anything looks different from what your invoice shows, ask support before paying.
  • Your receipt. Screenshot the transaction ID the moment the payment goes through. It is your proof if the payment ever needs to be traced.
